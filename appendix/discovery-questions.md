# Appendix 1: Prioritized Discovery Questions & Information Gap Analysis

This document outlines the structured discovery inquiry framework for **Client B (Structural Steel Fabricator)**. Because only two preliminary discovery calls took place—attended solely by an engineer from Cost Control (with a brief appearance by a colleague)—critical operational teams (Nesting, Detailing/Engineering, Yard, Procurement, Shop Floor, IT) have not yet been interviewed.

The questions below are ranked into:
- **P0 (Must Know Before Building):** Fundamental operational and data facts that dictate feasibility and core architectural decisions.
- **P1 (Needed During Pilot):** Constraints and workflow details required during model tuning, shadow-mode pilot, and system integration.
- **P2 (Nice to Know):** Long-term operational and strategic optimizations that can wait until post-pilot scaling.

## 1. Prioritized Question Matrix

| Priority | Category | Specific Question | Why It Matters | Current Working Assumption | Impact If Assumption Is Wrong |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **P0** | **Business** | What exact components constitute the reported 20–25% plate waste and 7–10% overall waste? Are torch kerf, skeleton margins, damaged stock, and offcuts all lumped together? | Determining baseline avoidable waste vs unavoidable process loss. If 15% is kerf/skeleton, optimization ceiling is much lower. | 22.5% plate waste includes ~3.5% kerf/skeleton, ~4.0% client revision scrap, ~6.0% unrecovered offcuts, and ~9.0% avoidable nesting inefficiency. | If unavoidable kerf/skeleton is >15%, expected economic savings are overstated by 40–50%. |
| **P0** | **Business** | What is the exact contract accounting rule for steel purchasing? Can raw plates or profiles be co-purchased or co-nested across projects, or does client contract audit prohibit shared steel? | Determines whether cross-project co-nesting is legally and commercially permissible, or if optimization must remain within single-project boundaries. | Steel is bought per project for job-costing, but fabricator owns the inventory risk; internal cost-transfer mechanisms can credit donating projects. | If contracts strictly forbid co-nesting (e.g. government/defense/cost-plus contracts), multi-project batching is eliminated, reducing savings by ~35%. |
| **P0** | **Nesting** | Why did the nesting team state that Strumis automatic nesting "wastes more material than manual"? What specific failures occurred when they tried it? | Reveals the exact uncaptured shop-floor constraints (e.g. poor remnant geometry, lack of thermal spacing, grain orientation, clamp interference) that manual nestings account for. | Strumis automated nesting produces irregular, unusable Swiss-cheese offcuts and ignores heat distortion/torch lead-ins, prompting operators to nest manually. | If the nesting team's rejection was purely cultural or lack of training, the solution may need less algorithmic customization and more UI workflow enablement. |
| **P0** | **Data** | Can the client provide 50–100 historical nesting jobs from the past 6–12 months in native Strumis/CAD format along with their actual cutting outcomes? | Essential for the Phase 1 Historical Replay benchmark. Without historical ground truth, we cannot objectively measure baseline vs optimized performance. | Strumis stores historical nesting runs, part geometries, and plate allocations in SQL database tables or archive files. | If historical nesting files were overwritten or not saved digitally, baseline establishment will require 4–6 weeks of live logging before optimization can begin. |
| **P0** | **Data / CAD** | In what digital format does Engineering release fabrication drawings to Nesting (e.g., Tekla Structures EJE/DSTV NC1 files, DXF, DWG, or flat PDFs)? | Determines whether geometry parsing can be fully automated via standard DSTV/DXF parsers or requires complex computer vision / manual CAD extraction. | Engineering uses 3D BIM (Tekla or SDS/2) and exports standard DSTV/NC1 files and 2D DXF profiles directly into Strumis. | If drawings are only delivered as flat 2D PDFs or paper blueprints, automated geometry extraction is blocked without an expensive human-in-the-loop CAD digitizer. |
| **P0** | **Operations** | What is the physical yard protocol for retrieving stored offcuts? When nesting calls for an offcut, how often is it actually retrieved vs rejected by the crane operator because it is buried? | If yard friction is high, nesting software will produce unexecutable cut plans. Offcuts that cannot be retrieved become phantom inventory. | Plates are stacked flat without location tracking; plates buried >3 deep are regularly ignored by shop-floor riggers under schedule pressure. | If yard access is completely unmanageable, offcut reuse must be turned off entirely in Phase 1, limiting optimization to prime virgin plate efficiency. |
| **P1** | **Business** | What is the average realized price per tonne for scrap steel sales vs prime plate/beam purchasing, and how volatile is this spread? | Directly impacts financial ROI calculation. We need net scrap loss delta ($/tonne) to price waste reductions accurately. | Prime steel = $950/tonne; Scrap salvage = $300/tonne; Net cost of scrap = $650/tonne. | If scrap salvage is higher (e.g. $450/t), net loss per tonne is smaller ($500/t), lowering total dollar ROI; if spread is wider, ROI increases. |
| **P1** | **Business** | How are client drawing revisions tracked and billed today? What percentage of scrapped parts from revisions are successfully recovered from clients? | Critical for the "Revision Scrap Recovery" value pool. Capturing revision scrap can generate hundreds of thousands in direct client recovery. | Revisions are handled informally; fabricators absorb significant scrap tonnage because they lack line-item proof of pre-cut parts scrapped by client revisions. | If the client already recovers 95% of revision scrap, this value wedge drops from $330k to near zero. |
| **P1** | **Nesting** | What are the mandatory cutting technology constraints (plasma, oxyfuel, laser, band saw)? What are torch kerf widths, plate edge margins, and heat dissipation rules? | Required to ensure that candidate nests generated by the optimization engine are 100% physically cuttable on the shop floor without warping or collisions. | Oxyfuel/Plasma for thick plate (4–6mm kerf, 25mm plate clamp margin); cold circular/band saws for profiles (5mm kerf, 0 degree square cut). | If thermal heat distortion requires complex part interleaving or cooling delays not accounted for, nests will cause warped parts and shop-floor rejections. |
| **P1** | **Nesting** | What constitutes the minimum dimension and aspect ratio for a plate offcut to be classified as "reusable" rather than "scrap"? | Parameterizes the objective function's remnant-preservation penalty (e.g. must be $\ge 1200\text{mm} \times 1500\text{mm}$ or $\ge 2.0\text{m}^2$). | Offcuts $<1.0\text{m} \times 1.0\text{m}$ or narrow slivers $<500\text{mm}$ wide are scrapped immediately; offcuts $\ge 1.5\text{m} \times 2.0\text{m}$ are stored. | If the shop floor refuses to store anything smaller than a half-sheet ($3\text{m} \times 2\text{m}$), remnant reuse opportunities decrease significantly. |
| **P1** | **Data / IT** | How accessible is Strumis's underlying database? Does Strumis have an open REST API, direct ODBC/SQL access, or must we rely on scheduled CSV/XML batch exports? | Dictates the data engineering architecture for data ingestion and two-way synchronization. | Strumis runs on a Microsoft SQL Server backend with standard ODBC connectivity and built-in XML/CSV import/export facilities. | If Strumis is a closed proprietary cloud silo without export capabilities, manual CSV file dumps will be required, increasing operational friction. |
| **P1** | **Operations** | How frequently do drawing revisions arrive after nesting has begun, and how does the nesting team currently halt or modify a job in progress? | Essential for designing the revision impact alert workflow so planners do not cut obsolete revisions. | Revisions arrive weekly on active projects; nesting planners receive email notices and manually cross-reference markups. | If revisions arrive daily on 80% of jobs, dynamic re-nesting and version lock become the highest priority system requirement. |
| **P1** | **Operations** | What is the inventory accuracy rate in the yard? How often is a piece of steel marked "in stock" in Strumis actually missing, damaged, or already cut? | Determines reliability of inventory-matching algorithms. | Inventory accuracy is moderate (~80–85%); discrepancies exist for plate drops and remnant sizes. | If inventory accuracy is <60%, software will allocate non-existent steel, causing immediate shop-floor stoppages. |
| **P2** | **Operations** | How are trailer loading and dispatch sequencing currently determined, and how does piece delivery sequence constrain cutting sequence? | Nesting must not violate erection/assembly sequence; parts needed first on site cannot be cut last unless yard has infinite buffer space. | Erection sequence is defined by project milestones, but cutting is currently batched by thickness rather than strict trailer load sequence. | If cutting must follow strict erection piece-marks to the minute, nesting grouping flexibility across drawings is constrained. |
| **P2** | **Logistics** | What is the root cause of low trailer utilization and high external equipment hire despite having 13 owned trailers and CarTrack GPS? | Investigates whether logistics scheduling can be addressed in a future phase once nesting and yard sequencing are stabilized. | CarTrack and booking systems operate in isolation; site delivery delays and uncoordinated factory dispatch create artificial equipment shortages. | Secondary issue; does not affect the Phase 1 nesting decision support pilot. |
| **P2** | **IT / Infra** | Does the client require 100% on-premises deployment due to IP/defense project security constraints, or is an enterprise hybrid cloud (AWS/Azure) acceptable? | Defines deployment architecture, containerization requirements, and CI/CD pipeline infrastructure. | Hybrid cloud is acceptable; drawings and metadata can be processed securely in a dedicated VPC or local on-prem agent. | If 100% air-gapped on-premises deployment is mandated, setup and maintenance costs increase by ~20%. |

## 2. Discovery Interview Plan for Uncontacted Teams

To close these gaps immediately during Phase 0 (Week 1), structured 45-minute discovery interviews must be conducted with the following stakeholders:

1. **Lead Nesting Programmer & Senior Operator (Call 1):**
   - Live walkthrough of a real nesting session in Strumis.
   - Screen-share showing why automated nesting was rejected.
   - Review of 3 recent plate nests and 3 recent beam cut lists.
2. **Head of Engineering / Detailing Lead (Call 2):**
   - CAD export workflows, Tekla DSTV file structure, drawing revision notification protocol.
   - Historical revision log audit: how revisions are marked up and tracked.
3. **Yard Manager & Crane Rigging Supervisor (Call 3):**
   - Physical tour/photos of plate yard and beam racks.
   - Review of remnant labeling, stacking depth limits, and crane unstacking bottlenecks.
4. **IT Systems Administrator & Strumis Database Owner (Call 4):**
   - Strumis database schema, SQL/ODBC permissions, API documentation, backup schedules.
5. **Procurement & Project Accounting Controller (Call 5):**
   - Project-based cost loading rules, mill purchase lead times, supplier standard dimensions, internal scrap credit transfer mechanism.
