DISCOVERY CALL NOTES - STRUCTURAL STEEL CLIENT
Two calls in the same week. Nothing has been proposed to them yet.

Client attendee: an engineer in Cost Control (a colleague joins briefly on
call 1).

How these notes were made: transcribed from the call recordings. Our side's
introductions, pitch, suggestions and case studies are removed. Our questions
are kept as "Q:". The client's words are lightly cleaned up for transcription
errors and are otherwise as said.

==============================================================================
CALL 1
==============================================================================

CLIENT:
"I have spoken to one other supplier. They told us they would suggest
different software packages for us to implement. In your case, would you only
develop the software and hand it to us?"
(We said we develop and deliver with our own team.)

"Have you gone through the Word document I sent? [We had the pointers, not the
document.] Honestly, even we don't have a clear idea of how AI can be
integrated into our company. In the last section I listed several areas in our
operations where we think AI could be used. We'd also like your expertise.
Once you have studied the company, tell us where else we could use it and how
we can improve the process."

Q: So you're looking for a consultation first, and then for us to develop the
solution?

"Yes, that's what we are looking for."

Q: Which area would you start with?

"If I have to pick one item, I'd pick nesting optimisation, the second point
I wrote down.

Everything we do is structural steel. We cut the steel, fabricate it and
deliver it to our clients. Before we fabricate, we have to cut the steel into
shapes. Nesting is when we do that cutting digitally, on a screen. They have
software that holds the shapes of the steel we order. They cut the steel
virtually, and then it goes to the factory, where the physical cutting
happens. So nesting is where we plan the cutting and the material digitally.

Right now our challenge is that we don't have enough data to analyse how much
steel is being wasted, how much of that we could have prevented, and which
materials are not being used. With an automated system we would know
automatically that an item could have been used in another drawing, on another
project. Right now a person does this. There is no software for
auto-allocation or anything like that. There are several things we could
develop there. That's a rough overview."

Q: Is the planning rule-based software, or manual?

"The planning is done manually, all of it. There are rules and conditions we
follow, but with software I think the automation and allocation would be
better."

Q: Roughly what percentage of material do you lose to wastage today?

"It depends on the type of steel. For plate material, the manual data shows
about 20 to 25%. For profiles it's 5 to 10%. It depends on a lot of other
factors, but on average you can take 7 to 10% overall."

Q: Do nesting plans change during a project, or is there one plan?

"We give a fixed budget at the start. A lot changes during the process, but the
budget stays fixed and they have to meet it."

Q: Any other areas you'd like to walk us through?

COLLEAGUE: "We have more areas: yard optimisation, optimising the loading plan
for trailers, and some factory operations."

CLIENT: "For the layout planning, you could focus on the warehouse: how we
store material today and how we could store it optimally. That's one area we
could look at."

"We'll send you links to our web platforms so you can get a sense of the
company and our operations."

(Asked for the next call in the same week: "I have to give my office an update
within this week.")

==============================================================================
CALL 2
==============================================================================

Q: Can you walk us through how material and a project move through the
company?

"The first team involved is business development. They bid for projects, and
the estimation team supports them by pricing each project. Once we win,
planning and engineering come in. Planning schedules the project: when it will
be finished and delivered, when material should be procured, when fabrication
starts. We give the client a completion date based on that.

Engineering releases the fabrication drawings. In our industry every structure
has drawings, and the factory works from those. Once the drawings are
finalised, they go to the nesting team. Material arrives from purchasing in
full sizes, so it has to be cut into the shapes we need. The nesting team cuts
it virtually in the software, and then it goes to the factory.

In the factory, the first department is sizing, which does the physical
cutting. After that comes fabrication, which does fit-up and welding to join
the steel into the required structure. Then comes coating: painting,
galvanising and fireproofing. Sometimes a job needs all three, sometimes only
one, depending on the client. Once coating is done, dispatch packs the
material and delivers it to the client."

Q: How many projects run at the same time?

"Around 10 to 15. Our capacity is about 80,000 tonnes a year, so roughly 7,000
to 8,000 tonnes a month. Depending on project sizes, we run more or fewer
projects."

Q: Is nesting done per project, or per day across all the projects that have
material in?

"It's done per project. Once drawings are released, we buy material for each
project separately. Each project has its own rates, so the material is released
for that particular project only."

Q: How often do drawings change?

"That's one area where we have problems. We get the initial drawing, and then
revisions keep coming. We don't have a clear record of that data. It's
something we're still working on with our current software."

Q: How is material stored once it arrives?

"We buy material according to the project timeline. If a project has to be
delivered early, we buy early.

For storage, we don't follow a proper sequence. When material arrives, it goes
into the store yard. Sometimes the material we need ends up at the very bottom,
so it takes longer to get out when it's needed. We don't store it according to
project requirements, and those requirements keep changing with the client
anyway. Right now a lot of projects are on hold because of the current
situation and shipment problems, so things change a lot. That makes planning
and storing material very difficult."

"One more point. When a loss comes from a client revision, we can claim it back.
But right now we don't know how many tonnes we lose to our own engineering
errors. That area needs proper data tracking."

"Nesting is something we already want AI to optimise."

Q: What software do you use?

"The CNC machines in the factory do the cutting. For nesting we use Strumis and
a PLM package, and we're moving more onto Strumis. Strumis works like an ERP
for us. All our data goes through it, and it has a nesting section. That's our
main software.

Nesting is done manually today. Say there's a full plate: they nest it by hand.
It's done by people, not by AI. We asked them why, because the software has an
automatic nesting option. The nesting team said automatic nesting wastes more
material and that they save more doing it by hand. We're not sure how accurate
that is, so we need that data as well."

(When sharing one purchased plate between two projects was raised:)
"When we buy material, its cost is loaded onto that particular project. All
our cost loading works that way. I'm not sure how feasible it is to split the
same material across projects. I'd have to check with my team. I don't know
much about nesting myself. I only see it from the outside."

"Right now, if half a sheet is left over, it's recorded as an offcut. When they
nest new projects, they check the offcuts first to see if anything can be used.
But I don't think they use the offcuts as well as they could. The idea is
already there, but it could be done better."

Q: Is your transport hired in?

"We have our own fleet: around 13 trailers, plus forklifts and mobile cranes.
When we need extra equipment, we hire it in. We also already have software we
use internally. There's a GPS tracker called CarTrack that records how long
each piece of equipment in the factory sits idle. Then there's a booking
system, where each department books a vehicle in advance and we track
utilisation. We brought these in over the last six months and haven't made full
use of what they produce yet. I only make reports from the booking system.

Our utilisation is very low. Even with all this equipment, we are still forced
to hire extra trailers, and management is very unhappy about that. We want to
reduce idle time, and we face a lot of problems in that area too. You could
look at our software and tell us how to use it better, or suggest other
software to connect with it."
